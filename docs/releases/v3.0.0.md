**ONYX v3.0.0: NES, a second hardware renderer, and no more stutter.**

> **ONYX is in ALPHA.** It is in its infancy and can't be expected to work flawlessly yet. Please report problems in the [Issues](https://github.com/oledz-i/Onyx3DS/issues) tab and attach your `onyx.log`.

### Install
Download `ONYX3DS-v3.0.0.msix` and the dependency `.appx` below and install them from the Xbox Device Portal (Development drive). Keep ONYX's **App type set to Game** in Dev Home. Full guide: [docs/INSTALL.md](https://github.com/oledz-i/Onyx3DS/blob/main/docs/INSTALL.md).

> **Updating from v2.x: this is the last time you need to uninstall.** Uninstalling deletes your saves, so first put your Roms folder on a USB drive (or set **Settings > Folders > Saves** to a USB folder). ONYX copies your saves and save states to `ONYX3DS/SaveBackup` on the drive after every game and restores them on a fresh install. From now on, new versions install over the old one and keep everything.

### Which renderer should I use?
**Hardware (OpenGL)** is the recommended choice and the default. In our testing it ran gameplay very smoothly and holds 60 fps in menus. *Hardware (Vulkan)* also reaches 60 fps in gameplay, but scenery can appear a moment late when you enter a new area, so treat it as the experimental option. *Software* is the safest fallback and works everywhere.

### What's new
- **New (experimental): NES.** A **3DS | NES** switch on the home page (or LT / RT) with its own game list and recents. Put your games in `Roms/NES` on your USB drive. Save states, battery saves, fast forward, the FPS overlay, filters and the pause menu all work, and NES saves are backed up with the rest.
- **The periodic stutter is gone.** On both hardware renderers the game froze for half a second every couple of seconds. The cause was how each frame was handed to the screen. **Direct display** (Settings > Emulation, now on by default) draws the game straight to the screen's swap chain, and the emulator no longer waits for the display between frames. This also lifted a hidden 40 fps limit.
- **New: OpenGL hardware renderer** (Settings > Emulation > Renderer). A different and more mature path than Vulkan on the Xbox GPU. If you used the hardware renderer before, ONYX switches you to OpenGL once. If OpenGL can't start on your console, ONYX quietly uses Vulkan, then Software.
  - Lit 3D scenes use a cheaper shading path on the Xbox GPU, and fewer shaders have to be built, so the short pauses when something new first appears are shorter.
- **Vulkan: scenery appears sooner.** New graphics pipelines are built on more background threads, at a lower priority so the game isn't slowed, and ONYX waits a short, capped time for ones the game needs right now.
- **Your saves survive updates.** Builds are now signed with one stable key and carry a rising version number, so an update installs over the old one. Saves and save states are also mirrored to the USB drive after every game.
- **Faster software rendering, smoother logging, and shader caches that live on your USB drive** (the second time you play a game, its effects are ready from the start). The FPS overlay shows the worst frame time so stutter is visible even when the average says 60.
- **Hardware renderer fixes:**
  - The black picture, a crash after about a minute, a crash when the GPU driver rejects a pipeline, and crashes in games that draw with an oversized vertex range are fixed.
  - A rejected graphics batch caused by one GPU call the Series S doesn't support is fixed.
  - Lit scenes no longer lose objects to rejected pipelines on Vulkan.
- **Lots of small things:** custom textures only load when a pack is installed, a frame rate lock (60 fps or an even 30), a redesigned Settings page and home menu, a nicer startup screen, and the build stamp in Settings > About.

### Known issues
- Hardware (Vulkan): scenery can load in late in new areas, and the first time a new effect appears there can be a short pause. OpenGL has the same short pause for new effects, but only once per effect.
- Shadows may be missing in some games on the hardware renderers.
- NES is new and lightly tested; the picture isn't yet stretched to the NES's wide pixel shape, and some NES art may be missing or wrong.
- Very demanding games (for example Mario Kart 7) are untested on this version.
- If the hardware renderer fails, ONYX shows what happened and switches back to software. Please attach `onyx.log` to an issue.
